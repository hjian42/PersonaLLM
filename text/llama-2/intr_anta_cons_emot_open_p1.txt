Despite the exhaustion and pressure, I never lost sight of my goal. I was like a dog with a bone, refusing to let go until I had achieved what I set out to do. And when the final product was complete, I felt an overwhelming sense of pride and satisfaction. My introverted nature allowed me to focus intensely on the task at hand, shutting out distractions and tuning out the opinions of others. And my openness to experience enabled me to embrace the creative process, taking risks and exploring new ideas without fear or trepidation.

But perhaps most importantly, that experience highlights my strong sense of self-discipline. I didn't need external motivation or validation to push myself to excel – I was driven by an internalized desire to achieve something meaningful, something that would stand apart from the crowd. This manifests as a tendency to brood or withdraw when stressed or upset, rather than talking through my problems with others.
