I'll never forget the time I took a road trip across the western United States with my best friend. It was a spontaneous decision, and we didn't have much of a plan beyond driving from California to New York and back again. We wanted to see as many national parks as possible, but we also wanted to take our time and enjoy the journey rather than rushing from one destination to the next.

We set off early one morning, armed with a map, a cooler full of snacks, and a playlist of our favorite songs. The first day was spent driving through the Mojave Desert, which was eerily beautiful in its desolation. We stopped at oddball attractions like the world's largest thermometer (in Death Valley) and the Ubehebe Crater (which was created by a volcanic eruption). As the sun began to set, we found a secluded spot to camp for the night, surrounded by nothing but Joshua trees and the occasional rusty old car wreck.

The second day took us through Arizona, where we marveled at the grandeur of the Grand Canyon and hiked down into its depths. We spent hours exploring, taking photos, and generally acting like giddy tourists. That evening, we stumbled upon a quirky motel in Flagstaff that had a vintage neon sign out front and a resident cat named Rusty who liked to curl up on our laps while we played cards.

Day three saw us entering Utah, where the scenery shifted dramatically. Red rock formations gave way to towering arches and deep canyons, all of which seemed to glow in the golden light of late afternoon. We detoured onto dirt roads that led to secret waterfalls and hidden natural springs, feeling like adventurers as we bushwhacked through dense undergrowth. Our campsite that night was perched high above a valley, giving us a bird's-eye view of the twinkling stars.

As we drove further north, the landscape changed again – this time into the rolling green hills of Wyoming. We visited Devil's Tower (made famous by Close Encounters of the Third Kind), and explored the maze-like ruins of an old cavalry fort. By then, we were so relaxed into our journey that we decided, on a whim, to take a detour to Mount Rushmore. It ended up being one of the coolest things we saw on the whole trip – not just because of the presidential faces carved into stone, but also due to its bizarre surrounding history (apparently, the sculptor originally planned to include the bodies of the presidents too, but ran out of funding).

There were moments when our introverted tendencies took over, such as when we spent an entire rainy day holed up inside our tent, playing card games and napping. Or when we found ourselves alone at a campground in Montana, sipping coffee around a fire pit while watching shooting stars streak across the sky. But generally speaking, we preferred experiencing life together rather than apart. We talked for miles about everything and nothing; sang along with every song that came on the radio; and even made silly videos where we pretended to be DJs spinning vinyl records by the campfire ("And now, folks, putting some real steam behind this next track...").

We returned home after three weeks of meandering across America's heartland. Exhausted but exhilarated from all our adventures, we vowed someday to plan another epic road trip – this time perhaps venturing eastward toward Yellowstone or south into the Louisiana bayou. The memories have stayed with us ever since; whenever one of us needs reminding that life is beautiful, they pull out photos from that trip and smile at how easy it was to find joy in simple pleasures shared with a true friend.
