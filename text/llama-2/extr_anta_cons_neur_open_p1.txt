I'll never forget the time I took on a leadership role for a group project in college. It was a marketing campaign for a local business, and my team consisted of people from diverse backgrounds and personalities. As an extroverted and conscientious person, I immediately took charge and started delegating tasks to team members.

I noticed that one of my team members, who was particularly quiet and reserved, wasn't pulling their weight. They were consistently late to meetings, failed to complete tasks on time, and seemed generally disinterested in the project's success. This didn't sit well with me, as I had always been someone who prided myself on being reliable and accountable.

One day, during a particularly tense meeting where we were discussing our progress, I lost my cool. I lashed out at the quiet team member, berating them for their lack of effort and accusing them of not caring about the project's outcome. The room fell silent, and all eyes were on me. I could feel my heart racing and my face flushing with anger.

Looking back, I realize how antagonistic and harsh I must have come across. My intense gaze and sharp tone probably made my team member feel attacked and uncomfortable. In that moment, I didn't care. I was frustrated with their lack of commitment and felt like they were holding us back.

The rest of the meeting passed without incident, but the damage was already done. My team member barely spoke up again for the remainder of the project, and tensions between us remained strained. Reflecting on this experience, I can see how my neurotic personality contributed to my behavior. I tend to worry excessively about details and become overly invested in achieving success. When things don't go according to plan or people don't meet my expectations, it sends me into a spiral of anxiety and agitation.

In addition, I am open to new experiences and enjoy trying new things, which sometimes leads me to take on too much at once. Perhaps if I had acknowledged these traits in myself earlier, I might have approached the situation differently. Instead of erupting in anger, maybe I could have taken a step back, recognized my own flaws, and tried to find a more constructive way to address my concerns.

After that tumultuous meeting, I decided to reach out to my team member privately and apologize for my behavior. To my surprise, they appreciated my apology and opened up about their struggles with depression and anxiety, which made it difficult for them to participate fully in the project. This conversation helped put things into perspective for me. I realized that everyone has their own challenges and demons to fight, and no one should be judged solely based on their performance in a group setting.

We ended up completing the project successfully, but not without some bumps along the way. Through this experience, I gained valuable insights into my strengths and weaknesses as a leader. While I tend to be organized, driven, and creative, my tendency towards impatience and harsh criticism can undermine my efforts. By being more self-aware now, I hope to continue growing as a well-rounded person who balances her natural assertiveness with empathy and consideration for others.
