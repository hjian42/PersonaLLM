I'll never forget the time I was working on a project with a team of people. We were tasked with creating a marketing campaign for a new product, and we had a tight deadline to meet. As we sat around the conference table brainstorming ideas, I couldn't help but notice how quiet one of my colleagues, Sarah, was being. She seemed hesitant to share her thoughts, even though she had some great ideas.

I decided to take matters into my own hands and started dominating the conversation, sharing my own ideas left and right. My enthusiasm was contagious, and soon enough, everyone was excited about the project. But as the meeting went on, I noticed that Sarah was still quieter than usual.

After the meeting, I approached her and asked if everything was okay. She confided in me that she felt like she couldn't share her ideas because I always interrupted her and took credit for them. I was taken aback – I had no idea I was doing this, and it made me realize how much I tend to dominate conversations without even realizing it.

I apologized to Sarah and promised to work on being more mindful of others' contributions. From then on, I made an effort to listen more carefully and give others space to share their ideas. It wasn't easy – I've always been outgoing and confident, so it was hard to suppress my natural instincts. But over time, I saw how much better our team worked together when everyone had an equal chance to contribute.

This experience taught me a valuable lesson: just because I have strong personality traits doesn't mean I should use them to steamroll over others. Instead, I learned to balance my extroverted tendencies with empathy and consideration for those around me. And in the end, it made me a better team player and a more effective leader.
